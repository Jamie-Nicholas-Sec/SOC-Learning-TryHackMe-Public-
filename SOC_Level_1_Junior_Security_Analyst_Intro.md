## Overview

I have started TryHackMe's **SOC Level 1** learning path to build practical defensive-security skills alongside my Cyber Security degree and professional IT support experience.

This introductory exercise helped me explore the responsibilities of a junior Security Operations Centre (SOC) analyst: monitoring security alerts, assessing suspicious activity, collaborating with colleagues, documenting findings and escalating potential incidents.

## Skills and concepts practised

- **Alert triage:** Understanding why some events require closer investigation and why an alert's severity is a starting point rather than a final verdict.
- **Authentication monitoring:** Considering the context around unusual login activity and recognising that suspicious behaviour may still have a legitimate explanation.
- **Threat-intelligence enrichment:** Learning how reputation data and historical reports can add context to an investigation without proving attribution by themselves.
- **Escalation:** Identifying the right SOC contact and recognising when an issue needs more experienced review.
- **Incident response:** Understanding the role of containment actions and the importance of following the organisation's authorised procedures.
- **Documentation:** Keeping a clear record of observations, reasoning and decisions so that another analyst can pick up the investigation.

## Key reflections

One of my main takeaways was the difference between **an indicator of suspicious activity** and **confirmed malicious activity**. For example, a location, IP reputation result or unusual authentication event can justify further investigation, but each needs context. An IP's registered network or geographical location does not necessarily identify the individual responsible for the activity.

I also saw how much effective SOC work depends on communication. Knowing when to escalate and recording *why* something looks suspicious are just as important as recognising the technical indicator. In a real environment, containment decisions need to follow the relevant playbook and permissions.

## Connection to my IT experience

In my service desk role, I already work with incident management, troubleshooting, identity and access processes, Microsoft Active Directory/Entra, MFA and escalation procedures. This exercise helped me see how those existing habits transfer into defensive cybersecurity: gather evidence, assess impact, communicate clearly and avoid unsupported assumptions.

## Next steps

I plan to continue through the SOC Level 1 pathway, focusing on:

1. Security monitoring and SIEM fundamentals.
2. Phishing, network and endpoint investigations.
3. Alert triage, incident reporting and escalation.
4. Practical exercises that help me explain my findings and decisions confidently.

My long-term goal is to apply for a trainee or junior cybersecurity analyst role after completing my degree.
